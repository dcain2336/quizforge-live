EMS Operations & Scene Safety

Scene safety comes first, always. You cannot help anyone if you become a patient yourself, and scene safety is the answer to more exam questions than any other single concept.

BSI (body substance isolation) applies on every call: gloves are the minimum, with more protection added as the situation indicates.

Mechanism of injury (MOI) and nature of illness (NOI) are different tools: trauma patients get an MOI, medical patients get an NOI.

Key Mnemonics: Patient Assessment

AVPU is a rapid check of level of consciousness. AVPU stands for Alert, Verbal, Painful, Unresponsive.

SAMPLE is the patient history mnemonic. SAMPLE stands for Signs and symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Past medical history, Last oral intake, and Events leading up to the incident.

OPQRST is used for pain assessment. OPQRST stands for Onset, Provokes, Quality, Radiates, Severity, and Time.

DCAP-BTLS is used during a rapid trauma exam. DCAP-BTLS stands for Deformities, Contusions, Abrasions, Punctures, Burns, Tenderness, Lacerations, and Swelling.

AEIOU-TIPS lists common causes of altered mental status, including Alcohol, Epilepsy, Insulin problems, Overdose, Trauma, Infection, and Stroke.

Vital Signs: Normal Adult Ranges

The normal adult heart rate is 60 to 100 beats per minute. Athletes can run lower, so treat the patient, not the number.

The normal adult respiratory rate is 12 to 20 breaths per minute. Count respirations for 30 seconds and multiply by two.

Normal adult blood pressure is about 120 over 80 mmHg. The systolic number represents perfusion pressure.

Normal SpO2 is 95 to 100 percent. Below 94 percent, consider oxygen per protocol.

Fasting blood glucose normally runs 80 to 120 mg/dL. An altered patient with low glucose should make you think hypoglycemia.

Airway, Breathing & Circulation

The airway priority order starts with manual maneuvers: head tilt-chin lift when there is no trauma, or jaw thrust when trauma is suspected.

Airway adjuncts come next. An OPA is used for an unresponsive patient with no gag reflex, while an NPA can be used on any patient who tolerates it.
